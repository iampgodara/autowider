Hyundai India has finally pulled the wraps off the much-anticipated 2026 Creta facelift, and the company isn't shy about the upgrades. After a lengthy teaser campaign that lasted nearly three months, the refreshed Creta arrives with a bold new face, a substantially upgraded ADAS suite, and — most importantly — a price that undercuts many expectations. The base variant kicks off at ₹11.00 lakh (ex-showroom, Delhi), making it one of the most competitive mid-size SUV launches of 2026.

What's New on the Outside?

The 2026 Creta's exterior redesign is the first thing you'll notice, and it's a confident departure from the outgoing model's polarizing look. Hyundai has adopted a more premium, road-presence-forward design language — one that takes clear inspiration from the global Tucson and Ioniq lineup. The front fascia now features a full-width LED light bar connecting the two slim projector headlamps, flanked by a wider, more aggressive lower intake.

The side profile gets flush door handles on top variants, while new 17-inch diamond-cut alloy wheels complete the athletic stance. At the rear, connected LED tail-lamps create a wide, distinctive signature that's sure to stand out in rearview mirrors on the highway. The overall proportions feel wider and more planted, giving the new Creta a visual solidity the previous generation lacked at this price point.

Upgraded Cabin: Finally Wireless Everything

Step inside and the improvements are equally impressive. Hyundai has replaced the old infotainment system with a dual 10.25-inch screen setup — one for infotainment and one acting as the digital instrument cluster — both seamlessly integrated into a single panoramic display housing. Wireless Android Auto and Apple CarPlay are standard from the S variant onwards.

The SX(O) — the range-topper — adds ventilated front seats, a 360-degree surround camera, and a Bose-branded 8-speaker audio system. The panoramic sunroof, previously optional, is now standard on the SX variant and above. Material quality has taken a noticeable step forward: the dashboard uses a soft-touch surface across the upper panel, and the piano-black trim has been replaced with a brushed metal finish that's significantly more fingerprint-resistant.

"The 2026 Creta doesn't just refresh a formula — it redefines what a mid-size SUV at this price should deliver. It's a confident, well-rounded package that's hard to argue against."
— Autowider Drive Review Team

ADAS Gets a Major Upgrade

Perhaps the biggest headline buried beneath the design updates is the leap in safety technology. The 2026 Creta now offers a proper Level 2 ADAS suite — branded as Hyundai SmartSense — on SX and SX(O) variants. This includes Forward Collision-Avoidance Assist (FCA), Lane Keeping Assist (LKA), Lane Following Assist (LFA), Driver Attention Warning (DAW), Blind-Spot Collision Warning (BCW), and Rear Cross-Traffic Collision Warning.

This is the most comprehensive ADAS offering in the segment, surpassing even the Kia Seltos which offers a more limited suite. In real-world use, Hyundai's FCA system intervenes smoothly and doesn't cause jarring false brakes on Indian highways — a criticism often leveled at rival implementations. The lane-centering works remarkably well on expressways, reducing driver fatigue on long journeys.

Our Verdict

The 2026 Creta facelift is less of a refresh and more of a thorough product improvement. Hyundai has addressed most of the criticisms leveled at the outgoing model — the dashboard feels less cluttered, the ADAS suite is genuinely class-leading, and the pricing remains sharp. If you're in the market for a mid-size SUV, the Creta has to be on your shortlist.

We recommend the SX variant at ₹16.85 lakh as the sweet spot — it gets you the panoramic sunroof, full ADAS, and wireless connectivity without the SX(O)'s price premium. The 7DCT gearbox is smooth enough for city use and responsive enough for highway overtakes. It's a car that ticks almost every box — and in a segment this competitive, that's no small feat.
